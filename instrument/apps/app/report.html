What you are looking at

Four cars, one surface engine. Nothing here is a mesh somebody sculpted — every panel is a Coons patch evaluated from an authored curve network at build time, and the normals in this viewer are the surface’s own analytic normals, not the triangles’. That is why a highlight travels down a flank instead of stepping across it.

Drag to orbit, scroll or pinch to zoom. Cyclorama is the light to SEE a car in; studio is the light to JUDGE a surface in — a highlight running down a dark flank is what a surfacer actually reads. Ghost makes the skin translucent and the structure solid, which is the only way to see both at once.

What the fillet solver did

The F1 is the first car built with soften — the variable-radius blend. It does not cut a corner off the way a rolling-ball fillet does: the patch edge stays exactly ON the authored curve, and the turn is packed into a band whose width sets the radius. So the seam is still watertight bit for bit, and the feature line can be tight at one end of a panel and washed out to nothing at the other — the wing crown runs R14 at its root to R70 where it dies into the deck.

The radius law is r = band · speed / (2 · break), derived from the peak curvature of the profile, with no fitted constant in it. Measured against what was asked, on a synthetic fixture, it lands within 1.5% up to a 40° break and 10% low at 90°.

On a real body the median is still 99% of the ask and the worst is 9290%. That is not the solver failing — it is one band being asked to track a break angle and an edge speed that each swing by an order of magnitude along the same curve. The answer is split-curve, which is already in the grammar and not yet built.

The one number that moved the wrong way

net vs evaluator reads 2.4e+3 mm on the F1, against a gate of 1e−9. Before the fillet solver it read 8.9e+1. Both are far outside the gate, but the cause changed and that is the part worth saying out loud.

The control net is the polynomial form of the surface — the thing a STEP file would carry. cellBezier builds it from the Coons construction and the cross-field, and it does not know about the compact quintic bumps that soften introduces. So on a softened cell it is no longer approximating the surface badly; it is describing a different surface. A number that is merely failing its tolerance is a to-do. A number that is structurally wrong and still prints is a trap, and the honest fix is for cellBezier to refuse — it already has a NotPolynomial refusal for exactly this — rather than quietly answer. Teaching it the new bases is the export stage’s work, not this build’s.

Nothing else regressed. Closed mesh is true on all four bodies, replay round-trips, the hash holds, and both honesty lints — no wall clock, no randomness, no raw transcendentals outside the numeric core — are at zero.

What holds by construction

G0 and G1 are not tolerances here. The patch edge is the shared curve, bit for bit, so two panels meeting at a seam cannot drift apart no matter how the body is edited — there is no sewing step and no sewing tolerance to blow. The medians in the tables above (1.6e−15 degrees) are floating-point noise, not a fit.

What is not by construction is G2. Curvature is a scalar, and no choice of polynomial coefficients makes two rational functions equal identically, so the curvature match is a fit — a very good one, a median three orders inside the tight end of the Class-A band, but a fit. The remaining G1 misses are all in the corner fade, where the correction is deliberately only part-applied so it cannot reach the next side along.

How this page is built

The photograph renderer ships thirty megabytes for one car: raw float positions, raw float normals, a full index buffer. This page carries all four in eight and a half, by noticing that most of that file is derivable or over-precise.

whathowcost
indicesnot shipped — rebuilt in the browser from the grid0 bytes, checked bit for bit at build
positions16 bits over the car’s own box0.033 mm worst
normals16 bits — NOT 80.0009° worst
materialone byte per cell, not per vertexexact

Eight-bit normals would cost 0.45°, and a clearcoat is a mirror: it shows a stepped normal as a visible band. That is precisely the banding the analytic-normal feed exists to remove, so it is the one place the compression does not economise.
