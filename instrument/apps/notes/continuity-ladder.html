The Continuity Ladder

What G1, G2 and G3 actually demand of a patch, what degree each one costs, and why the wobble everyone fears cannot reach this construction.

The question you asked, answered first

Neither, exactly — and the halfway house is the interesting part. The surface is a bilinearly blended Coons patch on chains of cubic Bézier segments, evaluated procedurally as S = S₀ + Φ + Ψ. It has never been a tensor-product B-spline object, there is no control net, and until an hour ago there was no degree elevation anywhere in the tree.

But the correction fields already are splines. Stage C1 made D*, a, λ and μ into cubic B-splines with adaptive knots — one to thirty-two spans per edge, chosen by doubling until the residual meets tolerance. @car/num carries Bernstein and B-spline bases, Cox–de Boor, and an equilibrated least squares.

So: a spline foundation without a spline surface. The commit this note documents closes that gap at the bottom — exact degree elevation, the Bernstein product rule, Boehm knot insertion and Bézier extraction, all of them identities rather than approximations. Everything below stands on that floor.

§ 1

What each rung actually demands

The usual framing — G1 matches normals, G2 matches curvature, G3 matches curvature rate — is true but hides the thing that decides the architecture: each rung is exactly one scalar equation per station along the seam. Not three, not nine. One. The derivation is worth writing down because the whole solver falls out of it.

Let patch A be parametrised A(τ,s) with s=0 on the seam, and B be B(τ,t) with t=0 on it. Both agree there: that is G0, and in this tool it holds bit for bit because the patch edge is the shared curve rather than a copy of it.

What this buys

Each rung is one scalar per station, and the rungs are sequential: solving G2 fixes α2 and β2, which are then constants in the G3 equation. So the ladder is a triangular system, not a coupled one. G3 can be added later without disturbing anything below it — which is not true of most surfacing schemes and is the reason this one can be built in stages.

Why G1 is free here and G2 is not

The tool does not solve the G1 equation; it makes the equation vacuous. Both owners of a shared curve read the same transverse field D*, and each builds its cross-derivative as

which needs C′ to lie in span{T*,D*} — a condition on the geometry rather than on our choices. Putting C′ in the slot makes it hold identically.

G2 does not have that structure. It is a statement about a scalar — II(ê,ê) — and no choice of polynomial coefficients makes two rational functions equal identically. That is why the P1 currently reads exact at G1 and 0.0015 % median relative at G2. The gap is structural, not effort.

The move that closes it

Do to G2 what C1 did to G1: stop fitting the natural field and choose the shared one. Prescribe a normal-curvature field κ* along each seam and require both owners to realise it. Then both sides carry the same second fundamental form by construction and G2 becomes exact, at the same cost G1 paid: the surface deviates further from the bare blend, and κ* becomes a design control rather than a derived quantity. It is fullness, one order up.

§ 2

Degrees of freedom, and the cubic–quintic–septic ladder

Your instinct is right, and it is right about a different object than the one this tool builds. Both answers matter, so here are both.

The textbook answer: 2k + 1

Take a bare tensor-product patch and ask it to meet Gk on all four sides. Control row j in a direction determines the transversal derivative of order j at that edge. Row 0 is the boundary, spent on G0. Rows 1…k are spent on the rungs, at each of two opposite edges. So:

The finding

G3 costs nothing in degree over G2. The composite bidegree is set by the correction fields and the corner window — the cubic splines a,λ,μ,ν, the shared direction D*, and the quintic smootherstep — never by the blend. Going from a quintic blend to a septic one raises a term that is nowhere near binding. So the G3 layer is a matter of solver work and one more field per edge, not of a degree upgrade.

The corner window is the expensive factor, and it is worth seeing why. It multiplies Δ by a quintic smootherstep that vanishes to second order at each end of a fade band, so every corrected term picks up five degrees on a fade span. Drop the window and the patch is (8,8) — but then §4's last limit bites, so the window stays until that is solved properly.

§ 3

Runge, and why it cannot reach this

The worry is exactly right in general and structurally absent here, and the reason is worth being precise about, because "high degree" and "wobble" are not the same thing.

Runge's phenomenon is a statement about interpolation at equispaced nodes as the degree grows. Its mechanism is the Lebesgue constant — the operator norm of the interpolation projector:

This pipeline supplies none of the three where it matters.

  1. No high-degree fit exists anywhere. The only least-squares in the tool fits the edge fields, and it fits them at degree 3, fixed. Residual is driven down by adding spans, not by adding degree — h-refinement, whose error bound for a cubic spline is ∥∞-clean and convergent: ∥f−s∥≤ 5384h4 ∥f(4)∥ . Each doubling is a factor of sixteen. The P1's worst edge went 24 → 1.8 → 0.047 → 0.002 mm on exactly that ladder.
  2. The composite degree is exact algebra, not a fit. Degree elevation is a convex operator, ci′= in+1ci−1 + (1−in+1) ci , and the Bernstein product rule has positive weights summing to one. Neither can introduce an oscillation the surface did not already have, because neither changes the surface at all. Degree 13 here is a representation of a surface built from cubics, not a cubic-count of freedom someone had to pin down.
  3. Variation diminishing bounds what is left. A B-spline crosses any plane no more often than its control polygon does. At degree 13 the surface still cannot wobble more than its net, and the net came from products of low-degree factors rather than from a solve.
Where it would bite, and the guard

Two places. First, if the finished patch were ever re-approximated to a chosen degree within a tolerance — which is what most kernels do on export, and which the ship gate forbids precisely because it trades the by-construction guarantee for the file. Second, in the field fit itself if the sample density were allowed to fall: three stations per cubic span interpolates them exactly and does as it pleases in between. That is Runge in miniature, we hit it — the span doubling stalled at 1.85 mm from 4 to 16 spans — and the fix is the floor MIN_POINTS_PER_SPAN = 8 that has been in the code since. Where degree must rise in a fit rather than in the algebra, the guard is Chebyshev–Gauss–Lobatto stations, τj=12(1−cosjπn), plus the fairing term of §4, which is a positive-definite penalty on exactly the modes an oscillation lives in.

§ 4

The curvature field solver

Everything above is local to one seam. The solver is what makes it global, and its job is stated exactly the way you stated it: satisfy the constraints everywhere while moving the surface as little as possible from what the designer drew.

The unknowns

Per shared curve e: the control points of De* (shared), and per owner X∈{A,B} the magnitude splines aeX,λeX,μeX,νeX at orders one, two and three. Plus, per cell, the interior coefficients that fullness already reaches at order zero. Collect them all in p.

The structural fact the whole solver rests on

With D* held fixed, S(p) is linear in every remaining unknown. Each correction term is a blend function times a magnitude spline times a fixed direction field, and a magnitude spline is linear in its own coefficients. So the fidelity and fairness terms are quadratic, the constraints are affine, and the whole thing is one sparse KKT solve — not an optimiser, not an iteration to a tolerance.

The functional

The system

The constraint block A carries only what cannot be made vacuous: the shared-curvature conditions of §1 and the corner compatibilities below. G0 and G1 contribute no rows at all — they hold by construction, which is the whole point, and it keeps A small and well conditioned. H is symmetric positive definite the moment w2>0, because the fairing term penalises exactly the modes the fidelity term leaves flat. That is §3's guard restated as a matrix property.

The one nonlinearity is D* itself, which begins life as a normalised bisector. Handle it by outer alternation: freeze D*, solve (11), re-derive D* from the new geometry, solve again. Two passes, then report the residual — the same discipline the corner fairing runs under, and for the same reason: a solver that silently iterates to convergence is a solver whose answer nobody can reproduce.

The limit that will not go away by solving harder

At a mesh vertex, G2 is generically unattainable. Going around an n-valent vertex, the second-order data of the patches must close up, and for a generic curve network the resulting linear system has no solution — the vertex enclosure problem. It is why the P1 reads G1 to 7.9×10−4 degrees corner to corner and still says "G1 holds, G2 never can" at a corner. There are four ways out and the tool currently takes the fourth:

  • Split every patch 2×2 so the vertex is surrounded by four times as many patches. Solvable, and it quadruples the patch count.
  • Raise degree and spend the extra rows on the compatibility. Costs the degrees §2 says are otherwise free.
  • Constrain the boundary curves so the compatibility holds by construction — the second-order sibling of the corner fairing already shipped at A11. Cheapest, and it moves the designer's curves.
  • Fade the correction to zero at the corner and report the band. What we do now. Honest, costs a small region of G1-only surface, and is the reason the corner window buys five degrees.

This is a genuine choice with a shape cost attached to each branch, so it is yours to make, not the solver's.

§ 5

The build, in dependency order

Each stage ships as capability a designer can reach, and the P1 changes only when you drive it. That rule has held for every stage so far and it holds here.

  1. Exact polynomial algebra. Done — commit 2a0f711. Degree elevation, the Bernstein product rule, Boehm knot insertion, Bézier extraction, subdivision, interval restriction; scalars and vectors; 27 tests, all identities.
  2. cellBezier — the control net. Assemble (7) with the algebra rather than sampling it. Gate: the net and boundaryCoonsPoint agree to 10−9 mm at a hundred stations per patch. This is the claim; everything after it is transport.
  3. G2 by construction. Prescribe the shared κ* instead of fitting the natural one, per §1. Gate: median relative curvature gap goes from 0.0015 % to zero away from corners, and the G1 probe still reads exact.
  4. The solver. (9) through (11), sparse, one KKT solve per outer pass, two passes and a reported residual. This is where "global" starts being true.
  5. G3. One more field per edge, a septic blend, no degree increase. Add the curvature-rate probe first — a rung you cannot measure is a rung you cannot claim, and row 4 of the audit has said "not measured" since the beginning.
  6. STEP. B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS written by us, read back through OpenCascade's STEPControl_Reader, sampled against boundaryCoonsPoint. Bound and verified as reachable in C0.
  7. Into the authoring shell. The verbs the solver needs — crown at order 2, tension at order 3 — beside the fullness control that landed at A12, with the bench's lenses live in the viewport so a change and its continuity reading arrive together.
What must survive all of it

G0 and G1 hold by construction rather than to a tolerance: the patch edge is the shared curve, bit for bit. That is not how Alias does it and not something a trimmed B-rep can promise. If sewing ever needs a tolerance, the guarantee has been traded for the export, and the trade is not worth making.
